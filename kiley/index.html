He votes for the district, not a party

Party breakdown on the House Clerk's site, Oct. 21, 2026. His words from his June 9, 2026 statement.

Voted yes

Social Security for retired teachers and first responders

71 Republicans voted no. He voted yes.

The Social Security Fairness Act ended two rules that reduced benefits for retired public servants. It passed and became law.

Roll Call 456, Nov. 12, 2024. H.R.82. Passed 327 to 75. Republicans 136 yes, 71 no. Democrats 191 yes, 4 no. Public Law 118-273. clerk.house.gov

Voted no

A party-line spending bill

Every Republican who voted said yes. He voted no.

In his words, “The House is giving away its most important power,” its yearly say over spending. He wanted reforms first, “like body cameras, training, identification, warrants, and nonenforcement zones near schools.”

Roll Call 214, June 9, 2026. S.2, Secure America Act, on passage. Passed 214 to 212. Republicans 214 yes, 0 no, 3 not voting. Democrats 0 yes, 211 no. Kiley, the one independent, no. clerk.house.gov. His statement the same day: kiley.house.gov

Voted no

The Republican majority's own rule

214 Republicans voted yes. He voted no.

Republicans on the Rules Committee brought this rule to the floor. He was one of only three in the majority to vote against it, and the rule failed.

Roll Call 60, Feb. 10, 2026. H.Res.1042. Failed 214 to 217. Republicans 214 yes, 3 no. clerk.house.gov

His story

From the classroom to the chair

He started his career in front of a 10th grade English class in inner-city Los Angeles. Today he chairs the House subcommittee over K-12 education.

Sources: kiley.house.gov/about and the House Education and Workforce Committee, which lists him as chair of the Subcommittee on Early Childhood, Elementary, and Secondary Education.
